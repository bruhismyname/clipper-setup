# Hook Formulas

The hook occupies the first 1-3 seconds. On TikTok, content performance depends heavily on whether these opening seconds retain viewer attention. A hook functions as an unanswered premise or gap that the viewer wants resolved.

## The Mechanism Test

Every hook should open one of these five cognitive gaps:

| Gap | Mechanism | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity** | Withholds the key answer | Promises a specific resolution |
| **Stakes** | Highlights loss, gain, or risk | Personal or financial consequence |
| **Dissonance** | Contradicts conventional belief | Counter-intuitive claim |
| **Self-relevance** | Direct address | Speaks to a specific scenario |
| **Tension** | Enters conflict mid-point | Unresolved dilemma |

## Extraction over Invention

The strongest hook is derived directly from the source video. The agent's role is to locate the pivotal line and place it first, rather than composing artificial promotional copy.

Example of artificial copy:
> "You will not believe what happened next."

Example of extracted premise:
> "I lost 40,000 dollars in one afternoon."

The extracted sentence is concrete, specific, and establishes stakes without exaggerated promises. The clip must resolve the premise within its duration.

## Nine Functional Structures

Use these patterns to identify compelling lines in the transcript:

1. **The Reversal**: "I thought X, but it was actually Y."
2. **The Specific Number**: "I repeated this process 1,400 times."
3. **The Non-Obvious Truth**: "The least understood factor in X is Y."
4. **The Loss Avoidance**: "I spent three years testing this mistake."
5. **The Challenge**: "Observe the outcome when X happens."
6. **The Admission**: "My initial assumption was wrong."
7. **The Direct Directive**: "If you currently do X, examine this first."
8. **The High-Stakes Choice**: "This single decision altered the entire business."
9. **The Direct Demonstration**: "Let us verify this with the recorded data."

## Hook Failure Factors

| Factor | Reason | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Starts with filler words ("so", "and", "um") | Delays core message delivery | Cut forward to the first substantive statement |
| Generic greeting ("Hey everyone, welcome back") | Redundant in short-form feeds | Remove opening filler entirely |
| Context dependency ("As stated earlier") | Incomprehensible outside the source | Select an independent section |
| Vague claims ("This is really important") | Lacks concrete information | Replace with specific numbers or concrete outcomes |
| Multiple disparate ideas | Divides viewer attention | Enforce one core idea per clip |
| Exaggerated promises | Causes immediate drop-off when unfulfilled | Ensure hook matches actual clip content |

## Three-Layer Alignment

A hook is effective when the spoken dialogue, on-screen text, and opening visual frame align:

| Layer | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Spoken Dialogue** | Exact words from transcript cut on clean word boundaries |
| **On-screen Text** | Maximum 7 words that reinforce the spoken dialogue |
| **First Frame** | Clear visual subject or speaker expression without empty backgrounds |

## Caption Writing Rules

The post caption provides algorithmic indexing and context for muted viewing:

- Reinforce the core premise of the spoken hook.
- Keep length to 1-2 concise sentences.
- Use 3-5 relevant hashtags (mix of campaign tag and specific niche topic).
- Avoid engagement-bait phrases that trigger platform spam filters.

## Verification Checklist

1. Does the clip establish context within the first 3 seconds?
2. Does the clip content fulfill the initial premise?
3. Does the segment function as a standalone narrative?
4. Is there exactly one primary topic per clip?
5. Does the on-screen headline clarify without repeating verbatim?
